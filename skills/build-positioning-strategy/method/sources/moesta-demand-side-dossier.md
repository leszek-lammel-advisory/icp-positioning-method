## Claims admitted into KB-6

| Claim ID | Synthesis | Operational consequence |
|---|---|---|
| MOE-C1 | Buyer demand begins in a particular situation where current progress becomes inadequate, not in a seller's feature list. | Diagnose the struggling moment before proposing an intervention. |
| MOE-C2 | Changing competes with the status quo through both attraction to progress and counterforces such as habit and anxiety. | Record each force separately and attach evidence. |
| MOE-C3 | Buyers pass through different progress states, so the same information need does not apply at every point. | Choose an intervention from the observed bottleneck, not a generic funnel stage. |
| MOE-C4 | Desired progress belongs in buyer language and cannot be replaced by a feature claim. | Reject feature exposure as evidence of demand. |

## Diagnostic method

The source supplies a causal map rather than a scoring formula. Start with the
**struggling moment**: what changed or broke, what current behavior became
insufficient, and why it mattered then. Reconstruct the **before state** and
attempted solutions. Then record four forces, each as observed, inferred, or
unknown: push from the current situation; pull toward a possible new outcome;
anxiety about a new approach; and habit or attachment to the current approach.

The agent must not complete the map from stereotypes. A buyer retaining a
spreadsheet does not demonstrate migration anxiety, preference for flexibility,
or a weak trigger. Those are separate hypotheses. The right output can be a
research request: ask what event made the spreadsheet inadequate, what the
buyer tried, what they feared changing, and what would count as progress.

KB-6 then locates the buyer in a progress state by behavior, not intent labels:
no recognized struggle; passive looking; active looking; deciding; first use;
or ongoing use. The point is not to classify a persona. It is to identify the
current bottleneck and the smallest useful intervention. A buyer who has not
recognized the problem cannot be served by a competitor grid. A buyer deciding
between options may need evidence that reduces a specific implementation risk,
not more feature descriptions.

## Worked reasoning

An operations lead reports missed handoffs after a cross-functional change but
has not investigated tools. The record supports a struggling moment and push;
the stage is passive looking if the observation is direct. It does not support
the belief that automation is attractive or that implementation is feared. The
next intervention is to confirm the desired progress and before state, not to
assert a comparison or launch a product demo. If the lead later requests
criteria for evaluating options and asks how integration works, the evidence may
support active looking or deciding, with a new bottleneck: evaluation clarity or
implementation anxiety.

## Limits

The force map does not predict conversion, quantify demand, select a market
frame, or prove a value outcome. It should not be used to attribute motives to
an account from firmographics, job title, website visits, or seller optimism.
M1 remains the authority for the alternative; KB-2 for evidence-bound value;
KB-5 for current perception; KB-7 for expression test results.
