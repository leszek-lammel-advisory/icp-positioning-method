## Method contribution

Perception and expression are different empirical questions. **Current
perception** asks what relevant buyers already associate with an offer before a
test or campaign. It needs audience language, comparisons, losses, objections,
or other market-facing evidence. **Expression validation** asks whether a
specific articulation of an upstream hypothesis is understood by the right
audience. It cannot establish that the underlying alternative, value, or market
frame is true merely because one sentence receives a preference response.

For KB-5, separate records into: unaided descriptions; named comparisons;
problem/category language; objections; desired internal narrative; and
ambiguous evidence. State a perception only at the scope the sample supports.
Repeated, independently supplied descriptions among relevant buyers can support
a bounded pattern. One memorable quote, a brand guideline, or the company's
homepage cannot.

For KB-7, write the upstream hypothesis first: the intended buyer, situation,
alternative, value-chain status, frame, and exact claim. Recruit a matching
audience. Ask unaided comprehension before asking whether the copy is liked:
who is it for, what problem is it addressing, what changes versus what, and
which proof is missing? Then distinguish a wording failure from a contradiction
in the upstream hypothesis. The latter is a research signal, not a license to
invent a new position.

## Worked reasoning

Ten relevant buyers read an expression and correctly identify the intended
problem but cannot say how the offer differs from their current workflow. This
may support an expression-revision hypothesis. It does not prove that the
alternative or value chain is wrong. Conversely, if the same buyers consistently
say the named alternative is not what they use, KB-7 should route to M1. If
they understand the difference but reject the claimed outcome as implausible,
route to KB-2 proof rather than optimize the headline.

## Limits

Preference is not demand, comprehension is not adoption, and a matched-sample
result is not an unrestricted market truth. The source supports a disciplined
testing process, not statistical claims without a stated method, audience,
sample, and decision threshold.
