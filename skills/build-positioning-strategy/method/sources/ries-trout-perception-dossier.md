# Source Dossier: Existing Positions and Buyer Interpretation

## Source

Public source: https://www.alries.com/positioning-articles

This dossier is original synthesis of a public article index and corpus. It is
not proof about any particular market's current mental model.

## Claims admitted into the domain

| Claim ID | Synthesis | Operational consequence |
|---|---|---|
| RT-C1 | An offer is interpreted against prior associations and category expectations rather than on a blank mental canvas. | A value claim must name its comparison context. |
| RT-C2 | The seller's internal view is not authoritative evidence of buyer perception. | KB-5 requires external audience evidence. |
| RT-C3 | A position can be unclear when a name or message invokes incompatible meanings. | KB-2 and KB-5 must expose contradictory expectations rather than resolve them by assertion. |

## Method contribution to KB-2

Relative value has a comprehension test as well as a product test. Even a
real capability difference may fail to become useful positioning when the
buyer is invited to compare it with the wrong alternative. KB-2 therefore
records the baseline explicitly and KB-3 later tests the comparison frame. The
agent may say that a chain is *plausible* when the comparison is supplied; it
may not claim that the buyer currently makes that comparison without buyer
evidence.

## Counterexample and limit

"AI-powered reporting" may be intelligible as a technical label but it does
not establish what a finance team currently does, what work changes, whether
speed matters, or whether the team believes the claim. Perception theory is
not outcome proof. It cannot turn a feature demonstration into an impact
claim, and it cannot select a category without evidence from the intended
audience.
