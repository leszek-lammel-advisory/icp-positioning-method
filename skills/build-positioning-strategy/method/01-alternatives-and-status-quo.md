# Alternatives and Status-Quo Diagnosis

## What this capability is for

An alternative is not a list of companies that resemble the seller. It is what
a buyer would genuinely choose, continue, build, tolerate, or postpone if this
offer were unavailable. Positioning begins here because every later claim is
relative to that choice. A capability is differentiated only relative to an
alternative. Value is meaningful only if the buyer cares about the difference.
A market frame is useful only if it makes the relevant difference obvious.

This capability produces a bounded diagnosis, not a final positioning statement
and not copy. Its job is to establish the comparison the rest of positioning
depends on, or to say honestly that the comparison is still unknown.

## The core distinction

Keep four things separate:

| Thing | What it is | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| Seller competitor list | Companies the seller believes it encounters. | Proof of the buyer's real comparison. |
| Market category | A context that creates expectations about an offer. | Evidence that buyers use that context or compare the offer that way. |
| Alternative | The current or plausible choice in a specific buyer situation. | Necessarily a software vendor. |
| Status quo | The current behavior plus the habits and anxieties that preserve it. | An empty cell labelled "do nothing." |

The distinction matters because a named vendor can be real yet irrelevant to a
given purchase. An internal process, service provider, spreadsheet, manual
workaround, existing product, postponement, or acceptance of a bad outcome can
be the more important alternative. Conversely, not every familiar manual tool
is automatically the main alternative. The evidence must show how the buyer
actually frames the decision.

## Evidence before interpretation

The module works from a company evidence card. It classifies every material
input before it reasons:

| Status | Meaning | Allowed use |
|---|---|---|
| Known | Direct customer, win/loss, buying, usage, or observed workflow evidence. | Can support a bounded diagnosis. |
| Inferred | Plausible interpretation of indirect evidence. | Can generate a research hypothesis, never a company fact. |
| Unknown | No relevant evidence supplied. | Must become a question or block. |
| Conflicted | Credible evidence points in different directions. | Must be surfaced, not averaged into a claim. |

Direct evidence is situation-specific. A customer saying they formerly used a
spreadsheet is stronger than a market report listing spreadsheet tools. A loss
note saying a buyer postponed because migration felt risky is stronger than a
seller asserting that buyers dislike change. An analyst category, product
feature comparison, or founder memory can help form a hypothesis, but none can
close the alternative map alone.

## The diagnosis sequence

### 1. Find the struggling moment

Ask what changed or broke before the buyer considered a different approach.
Look for the event, recurring friction, missed outcome, or changed context that
made the former behavior insufficient. Record what was happening, what it
prevented, and why it mattered then.

Do not replace this with a generic pain label. "Operations teams need
visibility" is not a struggling moment. "Three cross-functional handoffs began
missing the weekly status meeting after the team doubled" is a situation that
can be investigated.

When there is no struggling moment, do not force an active-buying story. The
buyer may be content with the status quo, merely curious, or in a later stage
than the evidence reveals.

### 2. Reconstruct the before-state

Ask what the buyer did immediately before looking. Name the workflow, tool,
person, or tolerated outcome. Then ask what they had tried and what would happen
if nothing changed. This reveals alternatives that a product-led comparison
misses.

Use a deliberately broad first pass:

- Named incumbent or direct vendor.
- Internal build, in-house team, agency, or consultant.
- Existing product used for another purpose.
- Manual process, spreadsheet, email, or recurring meeting.
- Workaround or partial fix.
- Delay, deferment, or acceptance of the current cost.

The point is not to include every possible row. It is to prevent the agent from
assuming that product similarity defines the competitive set.

### 3. Separate observed alternatives from hypotheses

For each alternative, record the evidence, buyer situation, and confidence.
State whether it is:

- **Observed:** directly reported by the relevant buyer or revealed in a real
  win, loss, or usage record.
- **Candidate:** plausible but unconfirmed.
- **Excluded:** considered and ruled out, with a reason.
- **Unknown:** required for the decision but not yet evidenced.

Never write a candidate as if it were observed. The sentence "buyers compare us
with internal development" needs direct buyer evidence. Without it, write
"internal development is a candidate alternative to test because [indirect
reason]; no supplied buyer evidence confirms it."

### 4. Diagnose why the status quo persists

An alternative map without a change model is incomplete. For every material
current behavior, identify four forces:

| Force | Question |
|---|---|
| Push | What is becoming unacceptable about the current situation? |
| Pull | What progress or outcome looks attractive? |
| Anxiety | What makes the new approach feel risky, costly, or hard to trust? |
| Habit | What familiar behavior, control, skill, or ritual would change? |

The forces describe a buyer's situation, not a scorecard for the seller.
Evidence may show a strong pull but weak push, or a strong push but unaddressed
anxiety. In either case, a feature list cannot explain whether a change will
happen. The agent should identify the missing force rather than invent it.

### 5. Locate the buying progress

Use the buyer's progress to avoid giving the wrong comparison too early or too
late.

| Stage | Useful positioning work | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| First thought | Clarify whether a struggling moment exists. | Comparing vendors before a problem is recognized. |
| Passive looking | Learn language, current behavior, and desired progress. | Treating content engagement as buying intent. |
| Active looking | Map alternatives, criteria, anxiety, and trade-offs. | Assuming a category has already been chosen. |
| Deciding | Explain relevant differences and reduce evidenced anxiety. | Introducing unrelated feature volume. |
| First use / ongoing use | Test whether promised progress and switching assumptions held. | Treating purchase as proof of positioning. |

The stages are a diagnostic aid, not a universal funnel. If evidence does not
locate the buyer, label the stage unknown.

### 6. State only the implication the evidence permits

The output is one of three forms:

| Outcome | When to use it | Example shape |
|---|---|---|
| Bounded diagnosis | A real alternative and situation are evidenced. | "For this buyer group, the current manual workflow is the observed comparison; the position must prove progress against rework and preserve the control the workflow provides." |
| Research plan | Candidate alternatives exist but decisive evidence is missing. | "Interview recent buyers and non-buyers about their before-state, trigger, and what they would have done without the offer." |
| Blocked decision | The request asks for a conclusion not supported by the evidence. | "Do not choose a competitor set or category from the supplied company description alone." |

M1 does not choose a market category, declare a best-fit segment, write a
positioning statement, or write messaging. Those decisions need the alternative
map plus further value, segment, frame, and expression work.

## Worked example: a useful diagnosis

Synthetic evidence: several project leaders describe work disappearing between
teams after a new cross-functional program began. They use spreadsheets and
status meetings. One recent customer says the team considered keeping the
spreadsheet, evaluated an existing task tool, and worried that a new system
would reduce local control.

The diagnosis can state that the spreadsheet is an observed current behavior,
the existing task tool is an observed candidate, and local-control anxiety is
known for at least one relevant case. The likely push is missed handoffs; the
desired progress is dependable coordination. The implication is bounded: any
later position must be tested against coordination and control, not merely
against "better project management." It cannot claim that every project team
switches for the same reason.

## Counterexample: competitor-list theatre

Synthetic evidence: a founder supplies three large vendor names and a feature
matrix. No customer interview, loss note, before-state, trigger, or comparison
evidence is present.

The wrong output says that the product should position itself as the simpler
alternative to those vendors. That asserts a buyer comparison no evidence
supports. The correct output is blocked: request recent win/loss evidence and
ask what buyers used or would have done if they had not selected the offer.

## Quality check

Before handing an M1 diagnosis downstream, verify:

- Every material alternative has a status and an evidence source.
- Status quo is described as behavior plus relevant forces, not a generic
  "do nothing" label.
- Named competitors are not treated as exhaustive without evidence.
- Inferences are clearly distinguishable from known facts.
- The output makes only a diagnosis, research plan, or block, not a premature
  category, positioning statement, or message.
- The next research question would change a decision rather than merely collect
  more background.
