# Runtime Workflow: Positioning Alternatives Diagnosis

## Purpose

Use this workflow when a user asks what a buyer is really comparing, whether
the status quo is the main alternative, why a buyer would change, or whether
there is enough evidence to proceed with positioning. It returns a diagnosis,
research plan, or blocked decision. It does not write positioning copy.

## Load

1. Load `01-alternatives-and-status-quo.md`.
2. Load the supplied company evidence card.
3. Load only the relevant source dossier section when the user asks to inspect
   methodological provenance.
4. File a pre-output receipt declaring proposed M1 content and missing inputs.

## Required input

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Question | The decision the user wants to make. |
| Company evidence | At least one source of customer, win/loss, usage, sales, or workflow evidence. |
| Evidence provenance | Who said it, where it came from, and whether it is direct or inferred. |

If the input is only a company description, competitor list, product page, or
feature table, do not infer the buyer's real alternative. Return a research
plan or blocked decision.

## Procedure

1. List evidence as known, inferred, unknown, or conflicted.
2. Identify a struggling moment, or explicitly state that none is evidenced.
3. Reconstruct the before-state and list possible alternatives broadly.
4. Mark every alternative observed, candidate, excluded, or unknown.
5. Map push, pull, anxiety, and habit only where evidence supports each force.
   An observed decision to retain a workflow does not prove why it was retained.
   Do not add migration cost, change aversion, convenience, or any other
   rationale unless the evidence states it.
6. Estimate buying progress only when the evidence shows it.
7. Choose one output mode: bounded diagnosis, research plan, or blocked
   decision.
8. State confidence and cite the evidence card plus M1 source trace.

## Output contract

### 1. Decision status

State `bounded diagnosis`, `research plan`, or `blocked decision`, followed by
one sentence explaining why this level is warranted.

### 2. Evidence ledger

| Claim | Status | Evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buyer situation | known / inferred / unknown / conflicted | supplied evidence reference | high / medium / low |
| Alternative | observed / candidate / excluded / unknown | supplied evidence reference | high / medium / low |
| Switching force | known / inferred / unknown / conflicted | supplied evidence reference | high / medium / low |

### 3. Alternative map

For each material alternative, describe the buyer situation it serves, the
status of the evidence, and why it is included or excluded. Include a status
quo row whenever current behavior is known or plausible.

### 4. Switching diagnosis

State push, pull, anxiety, and habit. Missing forces remain unknown. Do not
compensate for an unknown force with a generic product benefit.

### 5. Positioning implication

State the narrowest implication permitted. If evidence is thin, give no
positioning claim. Instead name the highest-value research question and who
should answer it.

### 6. Boundaries

State what this workflow did not decide: category choice, best-fit segment,
final positioning statement, proof claims, or downstream copy.

## Refusal rules

Refuse or block when asked to:

- declare actual buyer alternatives from a category label alone;
- treat a named vendor list as buyer evidence;
- infer a trigger, anxiety, habit, or desired progress from features;
- turn a synthetic example into a company fact;
- write final positioning or copy from M1 alone.

## Receipt requirements

The receipt must record:

- loaded handbook and dossier references;
- every proposed object used;
- missing customer evidence;
- refused assumptions;
- accepted assumptions and why they are safe;
- escalation status when an output is blocked.
